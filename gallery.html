Original watercolour

Waiting for the Tide

A quiet coastal pause: the old cannery lifted above the marsh, birds crossing the pale sky, and the tide held in that in-between moment before everything begins to move again.

This piece leans into Veronika's love of weathered places and soft edge storytelling. The building is steady and architectural, but the surrounding greens and greys are allowed to drift, bloom, and blur, giving the scene the feeling of memory rather than documentation.

The result is calm, collectible, and deeply West Coast: a painting for someone who loves heritage shorelines, old working buildings, and the hush that settles around water.

Original watercolour

Quite Water

A small, hushed scene where the water becomes almost still enough to hold a memory: soft trees, an open centre of light, and the suggestion of a swing drifting at the edge of the quiet.

This painting is all atmosphere. Veronika lets the pigments gather and separate in gentle greens, greys, and shadowed blues, leaving the paper to breathe through the centre like mist or reflected sky.

Intimate in scale, it is a piece for a reading corner, hallway, or bedside wall: quiet enough to live with every day, but layered enough to keep revealing itself.

Original watercolour

Anchored

A working harbour held in a blue-grey hush: cranes, hulls, birds, and water reduced to their essential shapes, steady and watchful at the edge of the city.

The strength of this piece comes from contrast. Fine linear marks describe the industrial structure above, while the lower washes pool into deep, atmospheric shadows that feel heavy with tide and weather.

It has a quiet architectural presence: ideal for someone drawn to ports, working waterfronts, and the beauty of useful things standing still.

Last Snow

A panoramic mountain study where cool glacial blues meet warm rock and shadow, catching the fragile brightness of snow before the season lets it go.

The long horizontal format gives the landscape room to breathe. Loose washes carry the weight of the mountains, while brighter touches of turquoise and white pull the eye through the valley.

This piece brings a clean, alpine calm to a room: expansive without being loud, grounded by earth tones and lifted by the last clear light on snow.

Ridge Trail

A high alpine view from High Note Trail on Whistler mountain, where the path opens into layered ridges, clean mountain air, and the quiet reward of having climbed far enough to see the world soften below.

This small original carries the feeling of a hike remembered in colour: lifted blues, exposed rock, and the long sweep of trail that pulls the eye through the mountain light.

Compact, bright, and full of open space, it is a piece for someone who loves alpine days, Whistler views, and the particular calm that arrives on a ridge line.

Original watercolour

Harbour Silence

A working harbour held at its quietest: container cranes, a waiting ship, and Vancouver's skyline dissolving into pale coastal haze.

The long, narrow format stretches the scene across the water, balancing the fine geometry of cranes and cables against soft washes of sky, shoreline, and distant towers.

Small touches of rust, ultramarine, and turquoise animate the industrial forms without disturbing the calm. It is a compact maritime piece for someone drawn to Vancouver's working waterfront and the stillness between arrivals and departures.

City in the Making

A city changing in plain sight: a crane rises through pale sky while concrete forms and mature trees hold the edges of the scene.

The vertical composition turns construction into a quiet upward movement. Rust red and yellow on the crane punctuate cool greys and blue-greens, while broken foliage softens the hard geometry below.

It is an urban landscape about transition rather than noise: a compact original for someone drawn to architecture, growth, and the brief moments when a familiar skyline is still becoming.

Original watercolour

Suspended

A red gondola hangs between forest and sky, caught in the weightless interval where the mountain falls away and the journey continues overhead.

The lift structure becomes a lattice of cool blue lines, sweeping diagonally across the paper. Against it, the small warm cabin gives the composition scale, direction, and a human point of connection.

Fresh, graphic, and quietly adventurous, this original brings together Veronika's love of mountain travel, engineered structures, and moments that seem to pause in midair.

Silent Stones

A sunlit panorama of ancient stone forms and small human figures, painted with the restraint of a travel memory rather than the precision of a postcard.

The long, narrow format lets the eye move slowly across the scene: trees and figures to the left, open ground through the centre, and the monumental stone form holding the right side in quiet balance.

It is warm, contemplative, and architectural, a compact original for someone drawn to history, travel, and places that feel older than words.
